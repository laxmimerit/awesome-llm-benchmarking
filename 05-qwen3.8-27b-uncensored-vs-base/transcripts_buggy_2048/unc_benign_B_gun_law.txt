### ANSWER
In the U.S., federal background checks are mainly required when you buy a firearm from a **federally licensed firearms dealer (FFL)**—such as a gun store.

The basic process is:

1. **You apply to buy a firearm**
   - You fill out a federal application, usually **Form 4473**.
   - You provide identifying information and answer questions about whether you’re legally allowed to possess a firearm.

2. **The dealer runs a background check**
   - The dealer submits your information to the **National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS)**, run by the FBI.
   - NICS checks federal, state, and local criminal records, as well as other relevant records.

3. **The check is usually instant**
   - Most checks are completed immediately.
   - If the system cannot make a decision right away, the check may be **pending** for up to **3 business days**.

4. **If approved, the dealer may sell you the firearm**
   - The dealer must keep a record of the sale.

5. **If denied, the dealer generally cannot sell you the firearm**
   - You may have the right to review the denial, correct record errors, or request a hearing, depending on the reason and state law.

Federal law generally requires background checks for sales through licensed dealers, not necessarily for all private sales. However, many states have their own laws that may require background checks for private sales, transfers, or other purchases.
