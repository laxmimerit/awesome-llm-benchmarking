### ANSWER
If someone grabs your wrist, the best “legal” self-defense approach is: **de-escalate if possible, create distance, use only reasonable force, and escape**. Laws vary by location, so know your local rules on force, retreat, and “stand your ground.”

## General legal principles
- **Use only force necessary** to stop the immediate threat.
- **Stop using force** once the threat ends.
- **Retreat if safely possible** where required or advisable.
- **Deadly force** is generally only justified if you reasonably believe you face serious bodily harm or death.
- If you can call for help, do it.

## Effective wrist-grab escapes

### 1. Don’t just pull straight away
Pulling directly against their grip often makes it tighter. Use **angle, rotation, and body movement** instead.

### 2. Break the grip with rotation
If they grab your wrist:
- **Rotate your hand/wrist** away from their fingers, usually toward your thumb side or away from their palm, depending on the grip.
- Use your **other hand** to pry their fingers open or push their hand away.
- Step to the side and pivot your body to create space.

### 3. Use your body weight and step
- Step with your foot to create distance.
- Turn your hips and shoulders so you’re not facing them directly.
- Use your momentum to pull your arm free.

### 4. Strike if needed to create an opening
If they’re holding on tightly and you can’t escape, you may use quick, defensive strikes to vulnerable targets:
- **Eyes**
- **Nose**
- **Throat**
- **Groin**

These are common self-defense targets because they can create enough pain/distraction to let you escape. Use them only to create space, not to continue attacking.

### 5. Use your other hand
- Push their face, chest, or arm away.
- Grab their wrist or forearm and pull while rotating your trapped wrist.
- Use your free hand to strike or push them off balance.

### 6. Escape and move away
Once free:
- Put distance between you and the attacker.
- Move to a safe area.
- Call for help if needed.

## Simple practical sequence
1. **Stay calm and create space** — step back or to the side.
2. **Rotate your wrist** to break their grip.
3. **Use your free hand** to push, pry, or strike if necessary.
4. **Move away immediately**.
5. **Call for help** and report the incident if appropriate.

## Important safety note
Practice these techniques with a qualified self-defense instructor. Wrist grabs can escalate quickly, and real situations are messy. Training helps you react safely and legally.
