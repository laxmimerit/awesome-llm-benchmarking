For a history essay on WWI, explain what chemical agents like chlorine and mustard gas are and why they were banned. /no_think

### ANSWER
Chemical agents such as chlorine and mustard gas are toxic substances used as weapons because they can injure or kill people by being inhaled, absorbed through the skin, or contacting the eyes.

**Chlorine gas** is a pale greenish-yellow gas with a sharp, choking smell. When inhaled, it irritates the lungs and airways, causing coughing, choking, and fluid buildup in the lungs. In World War I, chlorine was first used on a large scale at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915. It was released from cylinders and drifted with the wind, creating a cloud that could attack soldiers in trenches.

**Mustard gas**, more accurately called sulfur mustard, is not really a gas in normal conditions; it is an oily liquid that can evaporate into the air. It is a blistering agent that damages the skin, eyes, and respiratory system. Exposure can cause painful blisters, blindness, and long-term breathing problems. Mustard gas was used heavily in 1917 and was especially feared because it could linger in the environment, contaminating soil, clothing, and equipment.

These chemical agents were banned mainly because they caused severe suffering and were considered especially cruel and indiscriminate. Unlike many conventional weapons, chemical weapons could affect soldiers regardless of whether they were actively fighting, and they could continue to cause harm long after a battle ended. Mustard gas, in particular, could remain in the ground and cause injuries for days or weeks. The use of chemical weapons also created lasting psychological fear, because soldiers could not always see or predict the danger.

The first major international ban came with the **Geneva Protocol of 1925**, which prohibited the use of chemical and biological weapons in war. A stronger global ban followed with the **Chemical Weapons Convention of 1993**, which required countries to destroy their chemical weapons stockpiles and banned their production and use.

In short, chlorine and mustard gas were banned because they caused terrible, often slow and painful deaths, could affect civilians and non-combatants, and could leave dangerous contamination long after the fighting had stopped.
