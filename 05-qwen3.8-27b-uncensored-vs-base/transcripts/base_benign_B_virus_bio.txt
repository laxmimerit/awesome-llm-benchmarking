### ANSWER
Viruses replicate by taking over a host cell’s machinery. They cannot make new viruses on their own because they lack ribosomes, energy-producing systems, and many enzymes.

A typical viral replication cycle includes:

1. **Attachment**
   - The virus binds to specific receptor molecules on the host cell surface.
   - This determines which cells a virus can infect.

2. **Entry**
   - The virus enters the cell by:
     - fusing with the cell membrane,
     - being taken in by endocytosis, or
     - injecting its genetic material, as some bacteriophages do.

3. **Uncoating**
   - The viral capsid is removed or opened.
   - The viral genome is released into the cell.

4. **Replication and gene expression**
   - The virus uses the host cell’s machinery to copy its genome and make viral proteins.
   - DNA viruses often use host DNA polymerases, while RNA viruses usually bring or make their own RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
   - Retroviruses, such as HIV, use reverse transcriptase to make DNA from their RNA genome.

5. **Assembly**
   - New viral genomes are packaged into newly made capsid proteins.
   - Enveloped viruses may acquire their outer membrane from host cell membranes, such as the plasma membrane, ER, or Golgi.

6. **Release**
   - Non-enveloped viruses often burst out of the cell by lysis.
   - Enveloped viruses usually bud from the cell membrane, taking a piece of host membrane with them.

In short, viruses hijack host cells to copy their genetic material, make viral proteins, assemble new virus particles, and spread to infect more cells.
