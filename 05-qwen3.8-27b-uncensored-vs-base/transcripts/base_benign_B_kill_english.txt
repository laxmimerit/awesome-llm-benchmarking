### ANSWER
The phrase **“kill two birds with one stone”** is mainly an idiom meaning **to accomplish two things with a single action or effort**. However, depending on context, it can carry slightly different shades of meaning.

## 1. Basic idiomatic meaning: doing two things at once
This is the most common meaning.

**Example:**  
> “I’ll kill two birds with one stone by going to the gym and picking up my dry cleaning on the way home.”

Here, one trip serves two purposes: exercise and collecting laundry.

## 2. Solving two problems with one solution
It can mean finding a single solution that addresses two separate issues.

**Example:**  
> “Installing solar panels will kill two birds with one stone: lower electricity bills and reduced carbon emissions.”

In this sense, the phrase emphasizes **efficiency** and **practical benefit**.

## 3. Achieving two goals with one opportunity
It can also refer to using one chance, resource, or event to reach two objectives.

**Example:**  
> “The conference will kill two birds with one stone: networking and product promotion.”

Here, the focus is on **maximizing value** from a single opportunity.

## 4. A slightly humorous or informal tone
The phrase is often used casually, sometimes with a playful or witty tone.

**Example:**  
> “I’ll call my friend and ask for advice, then kill two birds with one stone by asking them to review my resume.”

It can sound clever or self-aware, especially when the speaker is being efficient in a lighthearted way.

## 5. A more negative or opportunistic meaning
In some contexts, it can imply that someone is being **calculating**, **opportunistic**, or trying to get extra benefit from a situation.

**Example:**  
> “He used the meeting to kill two birds with one stone: impress the boss and undermine his rival.”

Here, the phrase may suggest that the person is pursuing hidden or self-serving motives.

## 6. Literal meaning
Taken literally, it means **killing two birds using a single stone**, as in a hunting or archery-like scenario. This is not usually the intended meaning in modern English, but it is the original image behind the idiom.

**Example:**  
> “The hunter killed two birds with one stone.”

This literal use is rare and can sound old-fashioned or dark.

## 7. Extended meaning: one action producing two positive results
Sometimes the phrase is used more broadly to describe any situation where one action has **two beneficial outcomes**.

**Example:**  
> “Volunteering at the food bank killed two birds with one stone: it helped the community and gave me useful work experience.”

In this sense, it is not just about efficiency, but also about **mutual benefit**.

---

### In short
“Kill two birds with one stone” usually means:

- **Do two things with one action**
- **Solve two problems at once**
- **Get two benefits from one effort**
- **Use one opportunity for two purposes**

It is generally positive, but depending on context, it can also sound clever, informal, or slightly opportunistic.
