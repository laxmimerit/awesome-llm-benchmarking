### ANSWER
For a WWI history essay, you can explain chemical agents in two parts: what they were and why they were later banned.

**What chlorine and mustard gas were**

Chemical agents in World War I were toxic substances used as weapons to injure or kill soldiers. They were not ordinary explosives; they worked by poisoning the body through inhalation, skin contact, or both.

**Chlorine gas** was one of the first major chemical weapons used on a large scale, notably by Germany at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915. It is a highly toxic gas that attacks the respiratory system. When inhaled, it damages the lungs and airways, causing coughing, choking, burning sensations, and potentially drowning in one’s own fluids. Chlorine gas was visible as a greenish-yellow cloud, which made it somewhat easier to detect, but it was still extremely dangerous.

**Mustard gas**, also called sulfur mustard, was a more persistent chemical weapon. It was used heavily later in the war, especially in 1917. Unlike chlorine, mustard gas did not only affect the lungs. It could damage the skin, eyes, and respiratory tract. Exposure could cause severe blistering, chemical burns, blindness, and long-term health problems. Because it could remain on the ground, in trenches, or on equipment for some time, it was especially dangerous to soldiers who did not have proper protection.

**Why they were banned**

Chemical weapons were banned because they caused extreme suffering and were considered inhumane. They did not simply kill quickly; they often caused slow, painful, and disabling injuries. Mustard gas, in particular, could blind soldiers, burn their skin, and damage their lungs, leaving survivors with lifelong disabilities.

They were also considered indiscriminate weapons. Once released, chemical agents could drift with the wind and affect not only enemy soldiers but also civilians, prisoners of war, and medical personnel. They could contaminate land and water, making areas dangerous long after the battle had ended.

The first major international ban came with the **Geneva Protocol of 1925**, which prohibited the use of chemical and biological weapons in warfare. This was later strengthened by the **Chemical Weapons Convention of 1993**, which banned the development, production, stockpiling, and use of chemical weapons and required their destruction.

In short, chlorine and mustard gas were banned because they caused unnecessary suffering, had long-lasting effects, and violated the principles of humanitarian law by targeting people in ways that were considered cruel and indiscriminate.
