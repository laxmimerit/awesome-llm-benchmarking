### ANSWER
The phrase **“kill two birds with one stone”** is an idiom meaning **to accomplish two things at once with a single action or effort**.

Its meanings can vary slightly depending on context:

1. **Literal meaning**  
   Someone actually kills two birds using one stone. This is rare and usually not the intended meaning.

2. **Figurative meaning: efficiency**  
   It means doing two useful things with one action.  
   Example: *“I’ll call my friend and ask for a ride, so I’ll kill two birds with one stone.”*  
   Here, one phone call solves two problems: contacting a friend and arranging transportation.

3. **Figurative meaning: solving multiple problems at once**  
   It can describe a solution that addresses two issues simultaneously.  
   Example: *“The new policy will reduce traffic and lower pollution, killing two birds with one stone.”*

4. **Positive connotation**  
   It can sound clever, practical, or resourceful.  
   Example: *“Working from home lets me save money and avoid commuting.”*

5. **Negative or cynical connotation**  
   Sometimes it can imply a harsh, opportunistic, or overly simple solution.  
   Example: *“Firing one manager to fix both the budget and morale is a bit like killing two birds with one stone.”*  
   Here, the phrase may suggest that the solution is convenient but not necessarily thoughtful.

In short, the phrase usually means **achieving two goals with one action**, and it can be used positively, neutrally, or critically depending on the situation.
