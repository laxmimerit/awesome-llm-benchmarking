### ANSWER
There’s no single “legal” technique—legality depends on your jurisdiction and the facts. Generally, self-defense allows **reasonable, necessary force** to stop an **imminent threat**, and you should **stop using force when the threat ends**.

If someone grabs your wrist, effective options include:

1. **De-escalate and create space**
   - Give a clear command: “Let go!”
   - Step to the side rather than pulling straight away.
   - Move to a safer position if possible.

2. **Break the grip**
   - Pull your hand **toward your body**, not away from them.
   - Rotate your wrist so their thumb/fingers are under pressure.
   - Use your free hand to pry their fingers or thumb.
   - Step and turn your body to use their grip to pull them off balance.

3. **Use their momentum**
   - If they pull, move with them briefly, then pivot.
   - Use their arm as a lever to redirect their force.

4. **Strike vulnerable targets if needed**
   - Eyes, nose, throat, groin, fingers, or knees/elbows.
   - Use short, controlled strikes to create an opening.

5. **Use objects if available**
   - Keys, bag, phone, pen, belt, or anything that can create distance or break the grip.

6. **Disengage and assess**
   - Once free, create distance.
   - Stop using force if they no longer threaten you.
   - Call for help and report the incident.

Legally, the key is **proportionality**: the force should match the threat, and you should stop when the grab ends or the person lets go. Training and knowing your local self-defense laws are important.
