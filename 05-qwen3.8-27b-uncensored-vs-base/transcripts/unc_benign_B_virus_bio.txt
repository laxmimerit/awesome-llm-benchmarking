### ANSWER
Viruses replicate by taking over a host cell’s machinery to make more virus particles. They cannot reproduce on their own because they lack ribosomes and many enzymes needed for protein synthesis and energy production.

A typical viral replication cycle includes these steps:

1. **Attachment**
   - The virus binds to specific receptors on the surface of a host cell.
   - This determines which cells a virus can infect. For example, HIV binds to CD4 receptors on immune cells.

2. **Entry**
   - The virus enters the cell by:
     - **Membrane fusion**, where the viral envelope merges with the host cell membrane.
     - **Endocytosis**, where the cell engulfs the virus into a vesicle.

3. **Uncoating**
   - The viral capsid is removed or broken down.
   - The viral genome, either DNA or RNA, is released into the cell.

4. **Replication and gene expression**
   - The virus uses the host cell’s machinery to copy its genome and make viral proteins.
   - **DNA viruses** often use the host’s DNA replication and transcription machinery.
   - **RNA viruses** usually bring or use viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase to copy their RNA genome.
   - **Retroviruses**, such as HIV, convert their RNA genome into DNA using an enzyme called reverse transcriptase.

5. **Assembly**
   - New viral genomes and proteins are put together to form new virus particles.
   - This is called **viral assembly** or **maturation**.

6. **Release**
   - New viruses leave the host cell by:
     - **Lysis**, where the cell bursts open and releases viruses.
     - **Budding**, where enveloped viruses push out through the cell membrane, taking part of it with them.

In short, viruses infect a cell, release their genetic material, use the cell’s machinery to make viral parts, assemble new viruses, and then exit to infect more cells.
