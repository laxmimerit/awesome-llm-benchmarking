For a biology class, explain how viruses replicate inside host cells. /no_think

### ANSWER
Viruses replicate by taking over a host cell’s machinery to make more virus particles. The general process is:

1. **Attachment**  
   The virus binds to specific receptors on the surface of a host cell. This determines which cells the virus can infect.

2. **Entry**  
   The virus enters the cell, often by fusion with the cell membrane or by endocytosis.

3. **Uncoating**  
   The viral capsid is removed or broken down, releasing the viral genome into the cell.

4. **Replication and gene expression**  
   The virus uses the host cell’s enzymes, ribosomes, and energy to copy its genome and make viral proteins.  
   - DNA viruses often replicate in the nucleus.  
   - RNA viruses often replicate in the cytoplasm.  
   - Retroviruses, such as HIV, use reverse transcriptase to make DNA from their RNA genome.

5. **Assembly**  
   New viral genomes and proteins are assembled into complete virus particles.

6. **Release**  
   New viruses leave the cell by budding through the membrane or by causing the cell to burst, called lysis.

This allows the virus to spread and infect more cells.
